\reportsection{Source and scope}
The primary file contains 1,000 public Kaggle records that its uploader describes as synthetic. This project did not design or conduct a survey. No sampling frame, original item wording, generation algorithm, or within-night timestamps are provided. Results describe this file, not a sampled population. Source: \url{https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/harpartapsingh13/sleep-and-doomscrolling-habits-dataset}. Local source checksum and field caveats appear in \texttt{data/PROVENANCE.md} and \texttt{DATA\_DICTIONARY.md}.

\reportsection{Target validity}
A category-by-score check finds that 213 of 330 records labeled Poor have \texttt{sleep\_quality\_score}=5/5. The label derivation is undocumented. The classifier recovers a synthetic-file label; it is not evidence of detecting a sleep disorder in people.

An arbitrary sensitivity label, score $<5$, agrees with the Poor label in 75.5\% of 960 complete records (Cohen's $\kappa=0.355$). This exposes label dependence; neither label is clinically validated.

\reportsection{Exploratory association checks}
Five checks were specified after earlier dataset inspection; they are not preregistered. Pearson chi-square uses complete cases; Benjamini--Hochberg FDR adjusts the five comparisons. Cramer's V is descriptive. Significance alone cannot establish a protective effect.

\reportsection{Routines and negative news}
The unadjusted routine association is small. Reading does not stand out as protective: its Good share is below the 34\% file-wide rate. The no-fixed-routine group has the highest Good share. Negative-news exposure has a weak association with sleep category ($p\approx0.742$). These are descriptive contrasts.

An exploratory complete-case logistic adjustment ($n=953$) included age, gender, occupation, bedtime exposure, doomscroller label, negative-news exposure, night mode, and phone location. No routine term survives seven-term FDR adjustment. These contrasts cannot identify protective habits.

\reportsection{Model protocol}
The binary target is Poor versus Good/Fair. Measured sleep outcomes, quality score, and record ID are excluded. Imputation, scaling, and categorical encoding occur within model pipelines. Candidate comparison uses five outer and three inner cross-validation folds on 750 development records. A sigmoid-calibrated logistic ensemble and cost-weighted threshold were fit from development data. Earlier experiments may have inspected overlapping holdout rows, and the cross-sectional source cannot prove predictor availability at bedtime.

\reportsection{Internal evaluation}
Selected logistic model nested-CV balanced accuracy: 0.737 (fold SD 0.041). Historical 250-row holdout: balanced accuracy 0.760 (bootstrap 95\% interval 0.704--0.816), ROC AUC 0.825, PR AUC 0.720, Brier 0.155, sensitivity 0.723, specificity 0.796; threshold 0.335. These are internal label-recovery metrics.

Retrospective holdout calibration: intercept 0.149 (95\% CI $-0.231$ to 0.529); slope 1.218 (95\% CI 0.889 to 1.546). This is not external calibration.

The fixed-threshold logistic comparisons are diagnostic and are not directly equivalent to the tuned and calibrated nested-CV production estimate.

On identical development rows at the same threshold, full untuned logistic minus the simple doomscroller rule has balanced-accuracy difference $-0.019$ (stratified paired bootstrap 95\% CI $-0.052$ to $+0.014$). The full model's ROC AUC is 0.062 higher (95\% CI $+0.035$ to $+0.091$), but the binary rule offers limited ranking resolution. Incremental classification benefit is unestablished.

\reportsection{Independent context and limitations}
NHANES includes 8,040 analytic adults. MEC-weighted, age/sex-adjusted WLS with stratum-centered masked-PSU Taylor variance gives adjusted short-sleep systolic difference 0.82 mmHg (95\% CI $-0.86$ to 2.50; $p=0.316$) and diastolic difference 1.93 mmHg (95\% CI 0.67 to 3.19; $p=0.005$). Adult complete-case domain analysis remains approximate. PAD800/PAD820 are minutes per occasion; the activity comparison does not support a protective claim. PhysioNet subjects are unrelated to the Kaggle file. Neither dataset validates the classifier. High-exposure favorable-sleep cases are few; outcome contrasts are partly circular. External real-world model validation has not been performed.
